Why there isn't a true "Lusha for suppliers"

Contact data is relatively standardized: companies have employees with names, emails, titles, and phone numbers.

Supplier data is much messier because buyers care about attributes like:

manufacturing capabilities
machinery
production capacity
certifications (e.g., ISO)
quality systems
pricing
lead times
customer references
export history

Much of this information isn't publicly available or changes frequently, making it difficult to build a single, comprehensive enrichment database.

Opportunity for AI

This is where there is still significant whitespace. Imagine a platform that combines:

company enrichment (e.g., ZoomInfo)
trade data (e.g., Panjiva)
factory directories (e.g., Alibaba)
website scraping
certification databases
satellite imagery
news
customer reviews
LLM-based capability extraction

The result could be a "supplier intelligence platform" where a buyer simply asks:

"Find ISO 13485-certified CNC machining suppliers in Vietnam with experience producing medical devices, fewer than 500 employees, exporting to Europe, and capable of low-volume prototyping."

No single platform today delivers that level of AI-native, globally comprehensive supplier discovery and enrichment, although several are moving in that direction. This remains an attractive area for startups focused on procurement, supply chain intelligence, and agentic AI.
